## The searches were drifting from the offer

The account's visible search activity included supply shopping, repairs and cabinetry. Those searches called for something different from the custom tables the business wanted to promote.

The campaign needed clearer boundaries. Its reporting also needed attention: an attempt to submit a form was being treated as a completed inquiry. That could make a campaign look more productive than the available evidence supported.

## We focused the targeting and corrected the measurement

We reviewed the available search terms, paused generic themes and added exclusions for work outside the intended offer. The revised structure concentrated the active advertising on custom dining tables.

We also removed false submit-attempt events from conversion reporting and kept a labeled test out of the reported results. Campaign reviews recorded the changes, remaining tracking gaps and next checks.

## What the launch check confirmed

At the final launch check, the configuration contained:

- One enabled dining-focused ad group.
- Ten exact-match keywords and three phrase-match keywords.
- A daily budget unchanged from the previous setup.

The business had a defined campaign tied to the work it wanted to win, with clearer measurement rules and a written record of the changes.

## What this example establishes

This anonymized case documents a campaign configuration and reporting correction. It does not establish a subsequent increase in inquiries or sales. Those outcomes require continued measurement and feedback about the leads the business receives.

The useful first move was to make the existing budget more deliberate. Search terms, exclusions and conversion definitions all deserved attention before a larger spend.
