## The advertised next step was unavailable

The business had a booking calendar on its website, but that route could not accept new reservations. Visitors needed a contact path the business could actually use.

The owner preferred to discuss rentals by phone, including fit, timing and availability. The website needed to make that option clear.

## We brought the phone route into the main prompts

We changed the main website and article calls to action to direct phone prompts. Those links allowed visitors to start a call from the page.

We also clarified the displayed rental terms, including a rental period of up to 30 days. Visitors could find both the offer details and the contact option before speaking with the business.

## What changed on the website

The updated prompts linked to the business's phone number, with clearer rental terms alongside the offer. The client reviewed and acknowledged the changes.

The website presented the route the owner had chosen, giving visitors a usable next step while online booking was unavailable.

## The scope of the fix

This anonymized example documents the website change and client acknowledgment. It does not claim that the booking system was repaired or that the update produced a measured increase in calls.

A website review should follow the visitor all the way to the intended action. A clear button only helps when its destination supports the request the customer is trying to make.
