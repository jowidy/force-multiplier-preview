## The brief had grown broader

The client's expectations for website search results had evolved. The proposed first phase included broad planning and diagnostic work, including an analytics audit, alongside the search behavior the client wanted to explore.

We returned to the original request to define a focused first phase for the client's review.

## We made the proposed work more specific

We brought examples of the desired search behavior to the developers and reviewed the estimate against that scope. Together, we limited the first phase to an agreed test set and removed the broad analytics audit from that phase.

The delivery team assessed whether the narrower plan was executable. They confirmed a 30-hour estimate, with the next phase to be estimated after the first phase's findings.

## What the revised proposal established

The initial estimate moved from 60 hours to 30 because the scope had become more focused. The revised proposal was then sent to the client for review.

The client had a smaller first-phase proposal with clear boundaries and a delivery team that had confirmed the estimate. Decisions about later work could follow what that phase revealed.

## A smaller estimate for a smaller phase

This anonymized case concerns a revised estimate. It is not a claim that the same work was delivered in half the time, that the project was completed, or that a realized saving was recorded.

The practical lesson is to ask what the first phase needs to establish. Concrete examples, an agreed test set and a clear boundary can make a development proposal easier to assess.
