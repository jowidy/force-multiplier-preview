## Help the visitor make sense of the offer

A business may do excellent work while its website leaves basic questions unanswered. Which services are available? Who are they for? What does the work look like? How does someone begin?

We review the existing message and website around those decisions. The work can range from improving an important page to developing the content and design for a new site.

## What we can help with

- **Offer and service language:** explain what the business provides, who it fits and what a customer should expect.
- **Website structure:** organize services, work examples and supporting information around the visitor's needs.
- **Page design and copy:** develop useful service pages, campaign landing pages and other website content.
- **Proof and explanation:** shape project stories, practical articles and other assets from material the business can substantiate.
- **Contact routes:** review the path to a call, inquiry or booking and how it works on a phone.

Design and language need to support one another. A page can be visually polished and still leave a visitor unsure what the business is offering.

## Build from real material

Existing customer questions, approved project photos, service information and documented work are valuable inputs. They help the website sound like the business and show something specific.

We can organize those materials, identify gaps and develop the copy or assets the agreed scope requires. Client names, testimonials and project details should be used only where the business has permission and support for the claim.

## Define the website's practical job

Before settling the page plan, agree on what the visitor should be able to do. That might be understanding a service, comparing suitable project types or sending enough information for a useful first conversation.

The scope should identify the pages and assets to be produced, what can be reused, and the technical or third-party requirements that affect the work. Your role in supplying facts and approving the direction should be clear from the start.

## Start with the page that matters most

An existing site may benefit from a focused review of its homepage, a principal service page or a campaign destination. A broader redesign can follow when the structure and content need to change together.

Bring the current website and the questions customers keep asking. They are a useful starting point for deciding what the next version needs to explain.
