## Source context and interpretation

Promethean Research’s 2026 report states that 29% of agencies charge $175–$199/hour. This is a useful agency-level rate anchor, not a published role-by-role enterprise tariff. Source: [Promethean Research, Digital Agency Industry Report](https://prometheanresearch.com/digital-agency-industry-report/).

Clutch’s 2026 US digital-marketing band is $100–$149/hour, providing a lower broader-market comparator. Its directory mixes provider types and is not an enterprise-only rate survey. Source: [Clutch Digital Marketing Agency Pricing Guide](https://clutch.co/agencies/digital-marketing/pricing).

The role differentiation and selected low/base/high rates are professional modeling judgments informed by these anchors. “Enterprise-level” here means an established US agency delivering through accountable specialists and normal QA/account practices; it does not mean an enterprise-sized deliverable. The small-business scopes and phase-one hours are unchanged. The resulting base blends vary with the actual role mix; there is no uniform blanket $250/hour multiplier.

## Calculation and scope controls

For each scenario, fee = sum of each original workstream’s hours × its assigned client hourly rate. Low uses low hours and low rates; high uses high hours and high rates. These deliberately broad planning scenarios vary both delivery effort and rate position; they are not confidence limits or a claim that all agencies charge within them. The base figure is the recommended comparative-catalog estimate.

Mixed strategy/project-management workstreams in landing-page and homepage estimates use the strategy/director rate as a conservative blended proxy for that original combined workstream. Other channel coordination uses account/project management. Mixed production/QA work uses the operations rate. The JSON preserves every workstream and mapping so these judgments can be changed without rewriting the labor estimate.

Rounding is presentation only. Exact calculated fees, all role hours and effective rates are retained in the dataset. Effective blended rate is unrounded fee divided by scoped hours, including account time where applicable. Rounded displayed totals may differ slightly from sums of rounded components.

For a single recurring service, include program governance once: 2/4/7 hours, billed at $140/$180/$220, or $280/$720/$1,540 monthly. Common onboarding is 6/10/16 hours, totaling $945/$2,025/$3,960 once at the original account/strategy mix. For all seven services together, charge these amounts once across the bundle, not seven times. The dataset additionally shows equal one-seventh governance allocation per service for an all-seven catalog; this is accounting allocation, not a claim of identical management demand.

A one-off page or GBP/analytics project already has its own management/briefing hours. Do not add common retainer governance or onboarding to a standalone one-off. The price of a single SEO article or newsletter issue is a marginal production unit rather than a complete managed retainer; shared channel and account work still apply to a full standalone service.

A combined program versus seven separate contracts avoids six duplicate common monthly allowances and six duplicate common kickoffs. This is not a speculative efficiency discount. Further possible reuse of analytics implementation, social/ad creative or website components is not pre-discounted; apply a documented credit only to genuinely repeated work, as explained in phase one.

Media spend, software/hosting, image licenses, stock assets, paid distribution, third-party services and applicable taxes are excluded. No media budget was supplied, so a percentage-of-spend alternative is not modeled. These are agency service fees. Low paid-media spend may make the modeled service uneconomic without reducing scope; the model is not a recommendation to spend this amount.

No assumed minimum retainer, annual commitment discount, rush surcharge, procurement burden, insurance uplift or corporate compliance program is added. Those could alter a real quote, but were not requested. Agency profit is included in sell rates, not separately guaranteed or inferred from employee cost. Actual realized margin depends on agency economics and delivery performance.

## Scope summary

The unchanged phase-one assumptions govern all fees: 20 original social concepts across up to two networks; business-day capped community coverage; modest single-account Meta and Search-only Google programs; 900–1,200-word human-written SEO articles; templated newsletters plus active signup/list-growth work; daily site checks with a finite support allowance; and single small-business pages on an existing functioning stack. Normal revisions, production QA and channel reporting are included. Video shoots, large integrations, new brand strategy, full websites, unlimited community/support work and major technical remediation are not.

## Estimation assumptions and boundaries

### Common operating assumptions

- One small-business brand, one main geography/language, one website, one client decision-maker and reasonably prompt access/feedback. Existing business positioning and usable brand assets are available. Normal agency specialist roles may be held by the same person; count their time once regardless of job title.
- Base assumes a competent team, ordinary CMS/email/ad tools, reusable templates, consolidated approvals and professional client-facing work. Tools can help production, but no hours have been replaced by assumed autonomous AI output. SEO is human-researched and human-written.
- Low assumes excellent inputs and low friction. Base assumes normal briefing, coordination, production, review and correction. High assumes moderately difficult inputs, more execution/QA effort and fuller use of the stated caps. High is not unlimited service, a contingency reserve, or permission for enterprise transformation.
- Recurring creative: one consolidated revision round per batch/issue/article plus normal QA corrections. Website projects: two consolidated rounds, normally one on copy/design and one on implementation. A new direction after approval is additional work. Agency-caused corrections are included; repeated stakeholder reversals are not.
- Hours are pooled productive labor across staff. A senior strategist does not need to perform scheduling, and a developer does not need to own account reporting. Calendar waiting for approvals/verification is not labor. No generic agency overhead or sales effort is included.
- Discovery and reporting are concrete work, not percentages added on top of already counted hours. Monthly governance is a single consolidated meeting, preparation and cross-channel coordination. Channel reporting is the underlying channel-specific interpretation. If a client needs separate channel meetings or a board deck, add that scope explicitly.

### 1. Twenty original social posts

Base: 20 unique post concepts/captions per month across up to two primary social networks, with light format adaptations of those same concepts. This is 20 original posts total, not 20 for each network. Each includes designed or appropriately licensed/sourced imagery, brand formatting, alt text where supported, scheduling, calendar QA and monthly results interpretation.

Base visual mix: approximately 16 single-image executions and four simple carousels of up to four panels using reusable design patterns. No photography shoot, custom illustration campaign, original video/Reels, animation or influencer coordination. Client supplies factual product and offer information. ## Shared-work controls and how to adapt the estimate

1. The seven-channel program totals count common kickoff and consolidated client governance once. Do not sum seven standalone discovery meetings or seven copies of a consolidated report.
2. Each service retains its own genuine technical/creative setup. Shared brand discovery does not remove the work of building an email template or validating an ad conversion.
3. If GBP/GA4 setup runs alongside ad onboarding, identify reused baseline tagging and QA. A plausible combined credit is 4–8 hours once across Meta/Google/analytics, only if the same implementation/test evidence actually serves them. Meta pixel and Google conversion configuration are not interchangeable. The published totals do not pre-apply this uncertain credit.
4. If Meta creatives reuse approved social assets, reduce only the duplicated source-image/concept work; preserve paid-format adaptation, message changes and ad QA. A practical credit may be 1–3 hours/month, not the entire paid creative line.
5. Social posts promoting newsletter signup are counted in the social production volume. Email list-building time covers the acquisition mechanics and analysis. Paid list acquisition, if chosen, uses the already-scoped ad service plus any incremental campaign work.
6. A homepage and landing page delivered together can share discovery, component setup, header/footer and some testing. Identify these work packages explicitly; a typical modest same-site credit is 4–8 hours once. Do not mechanically discount both complete estimates without a build plan.
7. Maintenance smoke checks, campaign conversion checks and page-release QA have different purposes. Reuse the same test run when possible, but do not assume one removes all the others.
8. Do not apply all illustrative credits at their maximum automatically; some overlap. Record a single deduction against the actual reused work package.

## What would change the estimate most

Confirm actual channels and formats; community response coverage and maximum workload; campaign/market/feed complexity; SEO lengths and expertise requirements; email acquisition assets and segmentation; current website health and support SLA; existing CMS/design assets; approval rounds; and tracking/consent state. If these remain unknown, use the base assumptions as a planning model and validate them before a service commitment.

Rates, fees, margins, commercial markups, ad spend, software subscriptions, image licensing and other vendor expenses are deliberately absent from phase one. These hours are reusable inputs for phase two, not a backwards calculation from a desired selling price.
