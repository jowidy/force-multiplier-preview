# SMB versus enterprise agency selling prices

Same hypothetical agency. Same deliverables, quantities, production hours, revision allowances, QA, reporting and account-governance hours. USD, modeled as of October 18, 2026. Earlier enterprise figures are preserved exactly.

## Commercial recommendation

I would offer these as two clearly described delivery/commercial models, not price them solely from the client’s headcount. The SMB offer uses pooled, capable practitioners, senior review where specified, templated internal production workflows and a tightly scoped fixed-fee service. The enterprise card represents the agency’s standard senior-led specialist sell rates. Both deliver the same stated assets and service caps. A small business requiring the senior-led staffing model would receive that card; a larger client eligible for the pooled standard-scope service could receive the SMB card.

For this comparison, the only numerical change is the hourly sell-rate card applied to the existing workstreams. No hours are removed. The staffing explanation makes the two commercial offers plausible, but is a modeled business choice, not evidence that company size changes the work. The original enterprise price includes no newly invented procurement, security or compliance work to justify a premium.

The SMB card targets a lower realized price using a less expensive practitioner mix and a deliberately lower commercial yield. Rates still include overhead recovery and profit. No internal salaries, utilization or profit accounts are available, so neither offer’s exact achieved margin is claimed. An agency whose actual costs cannot support the SMB card should quote its normal rate rather than remove deliverables silently. There is no assumed annual contract, prepayment, bundle commitment or special discount condition.

## Why these rates are reasonable modeling choices

The broader US agency comparator is Clutch’s $100–$149/hour digital-marketing band. It is suitable context for a pooled-practitioner SMB offer, not proof of the exact service prices here. [Clutch Digital Marketing Agency Pricing Guide](https://clutch.co/agencies/digital-marketing/pricing).

Promethean’s 2026 report says 29% of agencies charge $175–$199/hour, supporting the higher blended-rate context used in the established enterprise-service model. It does not certify these role rates or segment prices by the scope of these 11 services. The comparison uses the same current rate context already checked for phase two. The precise role cards, staffing interpretation and prices are professional projections. They are neither agency quotations nor observed spend from an independent SMB-versus-enterprise client survey.

## Per-offering rationale and unchanged base scope

- Social: 20 unique concepts across up to two networks; approximately 16 single-image posts and four simple carousels. The SMB saving is a lower production/editorial rate mix, not fewer posts, stock-only imagery or omitted strategy/reporting.
- Community: 20 business days, capped at 100 routine inbound reply actions, 60 proactive comments and four partner shares. Both retain scheduled human coverage and escalation rules; neither promises immediate response or 24/7 moderation.
- Meta: one account, up to three campaigns/six ad sets, four simple creative variants, optimization and test interpretation. Same campaign/creative labor; no unpriced video or landing-page development.
- Google: one Search account, up to three campaigns/15 ad groups, four copy/asset variants and optimization. Neither version quietly adds or removes Shopping/PMax, feed work or new markets.
- SEO: 900–1,200-word human-researched and human-written articles, metadata, tagging, links, sourced imagery, publishing and QA. Two and four monthly volumes include shared content planning/reporting. No AI-first substitution or word-count reduction.
- Email: 400–700-word templated issues plus active list acquisition experiments/signup improvements, list hygiene and analysis. Both retain the list-building workstream; new lead magnets, bespoke acquisition pages and complex nurture programs are separately scoped.
- Maintenance: approximately 30 daily human checks, proactive updates, QA and detailed reporting, with four routine support hours in the base case. A lower SMB rate does not remove daily checks or convert support to unlimited coverage. Urgent incidents outside the allowance need additional scope.
- Landing page: a complete 6–8-section responsive page with copy, design, build, one standard form, conversion event, metadata and QA. Same outputs and review rounds, on a functioning site stack.
- Homepage: a fully developed 7–9-section small-business homepage with responsive header/footer, copy, design, CMS implementation and QA. Both are one homepage, not a full website or brand transformation.
- Additional page: 3–5 sections using an existing design system, including copy, build and QA. Contact versus richer About content still affects where work falls in the range.
- GBP/analytics: one location and ordinary GA4 installation/QA with up to three simple key events and relevant account links. Both exclude ownership disputes, complex ecommerce/server-side integrations and guaranteed Google verification.

## Optional SMB streamlining is not applied to these prices

If the buyer needs a lower budget, offer and label a different scope: fewer original visual concepts, lower community coverage, less ad-testing volume, fewer articles/newsletters, a smaller support allowance or supplied website copy. Removing all four base monthly common-governance hours would save only $540 at the SMB card and would remove the consolidated meeting/cross-channel coordination service; it would not justify a large unexplained discount. None of these reductions is embedded in the comparison.

Do not imply that an SMB should purchase all seven services. The full bundle is labor-intensive even at lower rates. If its price exceeds a small business’s budget, prioritize fewer services or clearly redefine scope. The estimate is not a recommendation to spend this amount.
